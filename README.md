# README for `Earthquake impacts on traffic safety using crowdsourced and police-reported crash data - Accompanying Code`  

`This repository contains 5 files of code which support the publication 'Earthquake impacts on traffic safety using crowdsourced and police-reported crash data'.`  

## General Information  

**Title of Dataset:**  `Earthquake impacts on traffic safety using crowdsourced and police-reported crash data - Accompanying Code`  

**Description of the Dataset:** `Research in support of regional earthquake early warning systems (e.g., the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) managed ShakeAlert® Earthquake Early Warning System) has revealed a gap in information about the impacts of earthquakes on roadway safety. This dataset includes earthquake shaking and roadway crash data from crowdsourced and police crash report sources used in this research project. This research integrated earthquake shaking data, crowdsourced navigation application data, and police crash reports to quantify and investigate crash rates after seismic events. This study includes over 400 earthquakes with magnitude (Mw) 4.0 or above within the contiguous United States since 2017. The Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) measure of shaking from the USGS ShakeMap product is used in conjunction with road network geometry in areas of potentially felt shaking. The MMI estimates at locations of crash reports generated by users and crash reports from California Highway Patrol (CHP) are used to quantify the frequency of crashes in earthquake-affected regions. Crash frequency per kilometer of roadway before and after earthquakes in different MMI bins are compared visually, and the influence of shaking intensity on crash counts is assessed using regression analyses including control periods from the previous year. Most crash reports occur in areas where shaking is MMI 3.5 or below, owing to the rapid decay of shaking with distance from the fault, leading to much larger areas impacted by lower shaking intensities. There are only marginal differences before/after earthquakes between the frequencies of crowdsourced or CHP crash reports in these low MMI regions. There are a few individual instances of crashes after an earthquake in regions experiencing MMI greater than 3.5 where there are few or no crashes in similar control periods. However, the number of reports in those regions is low, less than ten for some MMI bins, so it is not yet possible to draw conclusions from the results in high MMI regions.`

## Methodological Information  

**Description of methods used for collection/generation of data:** 
```
These data are used in this study to assess relationships between earthquake shaking and roadway safety. The data are connected primarily through geographic coordinates to produce a final dataset describing roadways impacted by earthquakes, as well as crash reports with associated ground shaking intensity measures:
```

| Data Type | Location | Provider | Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raster Shaking Data** | Centered on earthquake epicenter latitude and longitude | USGS ShakeMap Product [1] | Rectangular array in raster format of ground shaking values on the MMI scale [2] |
| **Roadway Links** | All road links within earthquake raster boundaries | OpenStreetMap [3] | Geographic properties of roadways within the earthquake affected regions. OpenStreetMap data are updated more frequently and provide better coverage of actual physical roads than any federally managed roadway geometry data |
| **Crowdsourced Traffic Navigation App Alerts** | Latitudes and Longitudes where alerts are reported by drivers | Waze for Cities, in partnership with U.S. Department of Transportation (USDOT) [4] | Alert types, locations, and other information for alerts reported by users of the application |
| **Police-Reported Crash Data** | Latitudes and Longitudes where crashes are reported by police officers | California Statewide Integrated Traffic Records System (SWITRS) [5] | Crash records reported by CHP statewide, including location, severity, and time of the crash, among other information |
| **California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Crash Reports and Crowdsourced Alerts** | Latitudes and Longitudes where crashes or alerts are reported | Regional Integrated Transportation Information System (RITIS) run by the Center for Advanced Transportation Technology (CATT) laboratory at the University of Maryland [6] | Alert types, severity, alert timing, and other alert-specific information for the Caltrans Crash and California Waze databases |

```
1.	U. S. Geological Survey. Advanced National Seismic System (ANSS) Comprehensive Catalog [Internet]. U.S. Geological Survey; 2017 [cited 2025 Sep 26]. Available from: http://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/search/ doi:10.5066/F7MS3QZH
2.	Wood HO, Neumann F. Modified Mercalli intensity scale of 1931. Bull Seismol Soc Am. 1931 Dec 1;21(4):277–83. doi:10.1785/BSSA0210040277
3.	OpenStreetMap Contributors. OpenStreetMap [Internet]. [cited 2025 Sep 30]. Copyright and License. Available from: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
4.	Driving Directions & Traffic Reports by Waze [Internet]. [cited 2025 Sep 30]. Waze for Cities: Real-Time Traffic Data for Smarter Urban Planning. Available from: https://www.waze.com/wazeforcities/
5.	California Highway Patrol. Statewide Integrated Traffic Records System (SWITRS) [Internet]. [cited 2025 Sep 30]. Available from: https://www.chp.ca.gov/programs-services/services-information/switrs-statewide-integrated-traffic-records-system/
6.	University of Maryland CATT Lab. Regional Integrated Transportation Information System [Internet]. [cited 2025 Sep 30]. Introduction. Available from: https://ritis.org/intro
```
